One clause per paragraph, not one topic per paragraph. Rhythm over density.

If the connection between two ideas isn't obvious from putting them next to each other, the fix is a better sentence, not a bridging clause like "the same instinct behind" or "which speaks to."

Cut aphorisms that sound written rather than said, related: [[on-writing-daily]] and [[john-darnielle-on-revision]] on process and revision as correction.

Wikilinks earn their place per-sentence, not per-note.

Fragments over hedges. Say the thing, stop.

Capitalize the first word after a colon when what follows is itself a full sentence or an instruction, lowercase when it's a continuation of the same clause.

[George Orwell's rules](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/): cut a word whenever possible, never use a long word for a short one, never use a metaphor you're used to seeing in print. Applied here less as commandments than as a filter for the second pass.
