One idea per note, self-contained enough to survive being read outside the context it was written in. [Luhmann's slip-box already worked this way](https://zettelkasten.de/atomicity/), but the [[zettelkasten|zettelkasten method]] frames atomicity as a discipline more than a rule, a compass rather than a litmus test.

[Andy Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes_should_be_atomic) writes that a note should be about one thing, but capture the entirety of that thing.

The practical test isn't length, it's whether a note holds up once it's pulled out of the piece it came from. [[stub-pages|Stubs]] grow into it over time. 

See [[evergreen-note-titles]] for what a well-factored atomic note's title looks like, and [[maps-of-content]] for what to do once enough of them accumulate to need an index.
