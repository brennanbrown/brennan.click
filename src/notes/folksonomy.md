Thomas Vander Wal coined the term "folksonomy" in 2004, a portmanteau of "folk" and "taxonomy", to describe the bottom-up tagging happening on Flickr and del.icio.us. No fixed hierarchy, just [users applying whatever labels made sense to them](https://vanderwal.net/folksonomy.html/index.html), with structure emerging afterward from the aggregate. 

He later split it into broad folksonomies, where many people can apply the same tag and popularity becomes visible, and narrow ones, where each tag is applied once, usually by the item's own creator.

Both [[symbol-taxonomy]] and [[theme-symbols]] are designed taxonomies rather than emergent ones, drawing from a closed list in `src/_data/categories.js` instead of coining a new tag per note.

But [[maps-of-content|MOCs]] and category pages both assume some structure will let a reader browse rather than only search, whereas pure folksonomy resists any structure not built by the reader themselves.
